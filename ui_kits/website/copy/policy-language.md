# Policy language

Policy pages are written in formal policy language. This part overrides the spoken-formal register in [voice and tone](voice-and-tone.md#voice) for those pages only. Every other rule in this skill still applies.

## Scope

Every page whose `page_type` is `policy`, and any page that states terms, rights, obligations, conditions or how personal data is handled. Examples: privacy policy, terms, cancellation policy, admission criteria, house rules, guest rights and responsibilities, ethics, accessibility, diversity, sustainability, accreditations, feedback and complaints.

Forms, buttons, cookie banners and other interface text on or around these pages keep the normal register.

## Register

* Formal and written, not conversational. The reader is a guest, a regulator or a lawyer, and each of them must read the same meaning.
* Precise over warm. No reassurance, hospitality phrasing, enthusiasm, taglines or brand lines.
* No contractions, rhetorical questions, exclamations or asides.
* "We" for Shantara and "you" for the guest are allowed. Name the legal entity (Metropolis Forward LLP) where the law looks for it, for example who is the data fiduciary or controller, or who the contract is with. See [naming and NAP](naming-and-nap.md#business-name-nap).
* Plain legal drafting, not archaic drafting. Keep sentences short. Avoid "shall", "hereby", "herein", "hereinafter", "notwithstanding", "aforementioned" and "said" (as in "the said guest").

## Wording

* Define terms in quotation marks on first use, then use them consistently: Shantara ("we", "us"), the guest ("you"), the "Policy", the "Website", named laws such as the "DPDP Act" and the "GDPR".
* Use the recognised term for the subject: "personal data", "processing", "data fiduciary", "controller", "legal basis", "retention period", "recipients", "consent", "withdraw", "booking", "cancellation", "refund", "admission".
* Use modal verbs with fixed meanings:
  * **must** / **must not** for an obligation on the guest or on Shantara;
  * **will** for a commitment Shantara makes;
  * **may** for a permission or an option;
  * **is not permitted** for a prohibition.
* State the actor in every rule. Write "We retain medical records for 5 years", not "Medical records are kept".
* State conditions and exceptions explicitly ("only where", "unless", "except where required by law").
* Use exact numbers, periods and names. Do not write "a while", "a few days", "usually" or "generally" unless the source policy says so.

## Structure

* Headings are noun phrases that name the subject: "Data retention", "Cancellation by the guest", "Visitors". Do not use questions, "How we..." headings or headings that tease.
* Open the page with one paragraph that states what the policy covers and whom it applies to.
* Lists of rights or obligations have a lead-in ("You have the right to:"), parallel items, a semicolon after each item and "and" or "or" before the last item.
* Use a table where a policy pairs items with values, such as retention periods or cookies.
* Before renaming a heading, search the codebase for links to its anchor. Keep any anchor that something else links to.

## Meaning must not change

Formal wording often adds commitments without anyone noticing. When writing or rewording a policy:

* Take every fact, period, condition and commitment from the source (the handbook, `content/`, or the current page). Do not add, remove, widen or narrow any of them. See [policy invention](health-claims-and-compliance.md).
* Do not turn "we try to" into "we will", or "may" into "must", unless the source says so.
* Do not add standard legal clauses (liability limits, governing law, indemnity, severability, force majeure) unless they are provided.
* Mark anything unconfirmed `[TO CONFIRM]` and do not publish it.
* When a change rewords the page without changing a practice, say so in the change log entry: "Reworded in formal terms. Our practices are otherwise unchanged."

## Rules that still apply

* No em dashes. No heading made of a title, a colon and a subtitle.
* "Guest", not "patient" or "client".
* No outcome guarantees and no isolated prices. Link to the tariff page.
* NAP, phone and email exactly as in [naming and NAP](naming-and-nap.md).

## Review

Before approving a policy page, ask:

* Could a sentence be read in two ways? Rewrite it so it has one meaning.
* Does any sentence sound like marketing or hospitality? Rewrite it as a statement of fact or a rule.
* Does every rule name who must act, and under what conditions?
* Are defined terms used the same way throughout?
* Has any commitment, period or condition changed from the source? If yes, revert it.
