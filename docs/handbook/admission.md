# 5. Guest Admission & Eligibility

Part of the [property handbook](../handbook.txt).

Knowing who Shantara can and cannot treat prevents staff from promising a stay to someone the doctors will later have to turn away. When in doubt, route the enquiry to a doctor rather than confirming or declining yourself.

### Who we treat

Shantara accepts guests with chronic, lifestyle-driven conditions that are manageable through naturopathic treatment, including diabetes, hypertension, obesity, PCOD/PCOS, thyroid disorders, digestive issues, arthritis, and stress-related disorders.

This list is **clinical eligibility**, not a marketing audience model. Internal ICP definitions (need-led customer groups for SEO, campaigns, content, and CRM) live in [ICP and marketing audience](../icp.md). Do not treat this admission list, or the programme names in [section 9](programmes.md#section-9), as ICPs.

### Who we cannot admit

Shantara does not accept guests with critical or acute conditions requiring emergency care, intensive medical intervention, or 24/7 monitoring. This includes recent heart attacks, unstable angina, stroke, severe organ failure, active cancer under treatment, or severe psychiatric conditions requiring hospitalisation.

Our doctors reserve the right to decline admission if a guest's condition requires clinical resources beyond our naturopathic treatment capabilities.

### Age policy

Guests below 18 or above 80 years of age are not permitted to participate in programs, due to the specialised therapeutic nature of the treatments and clinical protocols involved.

Minors aged 14 to 17 may be accommodated only as non-participating companions, and only upon a doctor's recommendation.

### Pre-consultation & health assessment

Reservations are confirmed only after a mandatory preliminary consultation with our doctors. Before this consultation, the guest must submit a Preliminary Assessment or Health Assessment Form (HAF), provide all relevant medical records, and disclose all current medications. We reserve the right to decline admission if naturopathy treatment is not suitable for the guest's specific health condition.
