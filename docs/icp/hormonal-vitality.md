## 11. ICP 05 Hormonal & Vitality Health

### A. Definition

People whose health changes relate to hormones, reproductive life stages, vitality, body composition, or energy.

**Women’s health need states:** PCOS, menstrual irregularity, perimenopause, menopause-related health, hormone-associated weight changes, reproductive wellness where clinically appropriate.

**Men’s health need states:** low energy, metabolic health, vitality, hormone-related concerns, men’s preventive health.

Do **not** split men and women into separate top-level ICPs until data justifies it. Campaigns and landing pages may still speak to one life stage at a time.

### B. Core job to be done

Spend a residential period under a doctor who will look at hormones, metabolism, sleep, and meals together, rather than chasing a single supplement or gym cycle.

### C. Recognisable problems / needs

| Kind | Examples (not claims) |
| --- | --- |
| Symptoms | Irregular cycles, hot flushes, low energy, sleep change, central weight gain, low mood, reduced stamina |
| Diagnosed conditions | PCOS, thyroid dysfunction (handbook), perimenopause, menstrual irregularity, hormone-related infertility investigations — only as the guest’s clinician has named them |
| Functional problems | Unpredictable months, stalled weight loss, feeling older than they are, training that no longer works |
| Goals | Steadier cycles or menopause transition, energy, body composition, a preventive men’s check-in |

Fertility is a handbook medical category. It is not automatically a marketed ICP promise. Reproductive wellness only where clinically appropriate.

### D. Trigger events

{Hypothesis}

- A PCOS diagnosis, or years of PCOS advice that did not stick
- Perimenopause becoming obvious
- A failed fertility round (handle with extreme claim care)
- Bloods showing thyroid or testosterone concerns
- Weight that shifted after a life stage and will not move
- A routine check-up that raised metabolic-hormonal flags

### E. Desired outcomes

Guests typically **hope** for more energy, more understandable cycles or menopause symptoms, and weight that is less stubborn. No promised pregnancy. No promised hormone “balancing” as a guaranteed biochemical result.

### F. Primary fears and objections

- Being talked down to, or being sold a women’s-wellness cliché
- Men fearing the stay will feel like a spa
- Privacy about reproductive health
- Whether doctors treat both women’s and men’s hormonal questions
- Interaction with HRT, thyroid medication, or contraceptives
- Time away during work and family years

### G. Purchase criteria

Clinical seriousness; whether women’s health is doctor-led rather than beauty-led; whether men are visibly welcome; duration; meals and activity; evidence; privacy.

### H. Buying roles

| Role | How it often shows up {Hypothesis} |
| --- | --- |
| Guest | The person whose hormonal or vitality concern is primary |
| Initiator | Guest; sometimes a partner |
| Decision-maker | Guest; couples may decide together if both stay |
| Influencer | Gynaecologist, endocrinologist, friend with PCOS or menopause experience |
| Companion | Partner, quite often |

### I. Search and discovery behaviour

{Seed terms requiring research}

**Problem-led hypotheses:** PCOS, irregular periods, menopause, perimenopause, hormonal weight gain, low energy men, low testosterone (validate carefully; do not medicalise ads).

**Solution-led hypotheses:** PCOS retreat, menopause retreat, women’s health naturopathy, men’s vitality retreat.

**Question-led research:** can a residential stay help PCOS, what to eat in perimenopause, naturopathy and thyroid medication.

**Branded / comparison intent:** Shantara PCOS, Shantara women’s health; beauty and spa SERPs will pollute this space.

### J. Search intent stages

Awareness (body has changed) → problem research (PCOS, menopause, vitality) → solution research → comparison (clinic vs retreat vs spa) → commercial intent → brand validation → consultation → booking.

### K. Content requirements

Territories: PCOS in plain language; perimenopause and menopause as health, not anti-ageing marketing; men’s vitality without clinic-bro clichés; medication; who the doctors are; when fertility content is honest enough to publish. Do not run a “hormone reset” slogan.

### L. Evidence required

Clinician review; doctor credentials; protocol explanation; safety / eligibility; external evidence for endocrine claims; patient experience without fertility-success advertising unless separately approved with evidence and legal review.

### M. Programme associations

Indicative: Hormonal & Endocrine Balance; Men’s Vitality (named handbook wellness programme); Weight Management; Reproductive & Fertility Optimization where appropriate. Men’s Vitality is a programme name, not a seventh ICP — ICP 05 still owns the need. A guest may be placed on a different programme after assessment.

### N. Appropriate CTA

Book a Consultation; Meet our doctors; Learn about the programme. Avoid “Balance your hormones now”.

### O. Conversion objective

Qualified enquiry, often from a condition page (PCOS, menopause) rather than a programme name.

### P. Exclusions / poor fit

Marketing poor-fit: beauty-retreat traffic, anti-ageing injectables audience, guaranteed fertility shoppers. Clinical exclusions remain handbook-wide. Do not write marketing rules about who with PCOS or thyroid disease may come.
