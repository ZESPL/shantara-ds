## 8. ICP 02 Pain, Mobility & Rehabilitation

### A. Definition

People whose pain or loss of mobility is materially affecting daily life, and who are looking for structured rehabilitation or non-surgical supportive management where that is appropriate.

### B. Core job to be done

Get a supervised period of assessment, movement, rest, and therapies that might reduce the day-to-day tax of pain, without pretending surgery or medication is never needed.

### C. Recognisable problems / needs

| Kind | Examples (not claims) |
| --- | --- |
| Symptoms | Persistent pain, stiffness, numbness, poor sleep because of pain, swelling |
| Diagnosed conditions | Osteoarthritis, other arthritis, spondylosis, disc-related pain, frozen shoulder, sciatica, gout, fibromyalgia (as named by their clinician) |
| Functional problems | Cannot sit through work, cannot walk their usual distance, difficulty dressing or climbing stairs, fear of becoming dependent |
| Goals | Move with less fear, return to ordinary tasks, understand what to do at home, delay or complement surgical pathways where a doctor agrees |

Do not imply that Shantara replaces orthopaedic or rheumatology care. “Non-surgical” is a hope some guests bring; it is not a promise.

### D. Trigger events

{Hypothesis}

- Pain that has lasted months despite outpatient physiotherapy
- A scan result (disc, knee, shoulder) and a wish to try conservative care
- Surgery recommended, guest wants a second path or prehab
- Pain starting to limit work or care for family
- A flare after travel or a period of inactivity
- A previous retreat or hospital stay that helped temporarily

### E. Desired outcomes

Guests typically **hope** for less day-to-day pain, better range of movement, confidence to walk or work, and a plan they can continue. Pain reduction is not guaranteed. Imaging “fixing” is not a claim.

### F. Primary fears and objections

- “Will they make it worse?”
- “Is this real rehab or massage?”
- Time away versus an outpatient clinic near home
- Whether doctors understand their specific joint or spine issue
- Need to continue existing pain medication
- Accessibility of rooms, grounds, and treatment spaces
- Scepticism of naturopathy for structural problems

### G. Purchase criteria

Named clinical oversight; physiotherapy / movement competence if offered; typical duration for rehab; honesty about what cannot be treated; accessibility; companion policy; evidence of similar cases without promising the same result.

### H. Buying roles

| Role | How it often shows up {Hypothesis} |
| --- | --- |
| Guest | The person in pain |
| Initiator | Often an adult child or spouse for older guests; the guest themselves for working-age musculoskeletal pain |
| Decision-maker | Frequently shared with family when mobility and travel are hard |
| Influencer | Orthopaedist, physiotherapist, previous guest |
| Companion | Common; may be required for support. Companion is not automatically a guest on the same programme |

### I. Search and discovery behaviour

{Seed terms requiring research}

**Problem-led hypotheses:** chronic back pain, sciatica, frozen shoulder, knee pain, neck pain, arthritis pain, can’t walk far.

**Solution-led hypotheses:** back pain retreat, arthritis naturopathy, non-surgical disc treatment, residential physiotherapy, pain rehabilitation India.

**Question-led research:** can naturopathy help sciatica, how long is rehab for frozen shoulder, should I try a retreat before knee replacement.

**Branded / comparison intent:** Shantara arthritis, Shantara back pain; comparison with hospitals, Ayurvedic centres, and spa “pain relief” packages (the last is poor-fit traffic).

### J. Search intent stages

Awareness (pain becoming unignorable) → problem research (condition and region) → solution research (surgery vs conservative vs retreat) → comparison (clinical seriousness) → high commercial intent (programme / duration) → brand validation (doctors, facilities) → consultation → booking after assessment.

### K. Content requirements

Territories: living with chronic back or joint pain; what assessment looks like; movement and rest in a residential day; who is not a candidate; relationship to conventional care; accessibility; companion stays. Avoid miracle musculoskeletal before-and-afters.

### L. Evidence required

Clinician review; protocol explanation; facility capability (treatment rooms, movement spaces); safety / eligibility; doctor credentials; patient experience of function and stay quality, not of “curing arthritis”. External evidence if a mechanism is described. Original outcomes data only if collected and approved.

### M. Programme associations

Indicative: Inflammatory & Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation; Complete Healing; therapies a doctor may include (for example acupuncture or massage as part of a plan, not as a menu). Cardiac recovery for medically stable guests sits primarily with ICP 06 and handbook Heart & Circulatory Health, not automatically here.

### N. Appropriate CTA

Read what to expect; Meet our doctors; Book a Consultation. Do not use “Book your pain-free life”.

### O. Conversion objective

Qualified enquiry with enough context to triage musculoskeletal complexity. Consultation before any implied programme match.

### P. Exclusions / poor fit

Marketing poor-fit: massage-only seekers, emergency acute injury, people wanting a guaranteed alternative to indicated surgery. Handbook exclusions cover acute / critical care. Do not invent a list of untreatable joints without a clinical source.
