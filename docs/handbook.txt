## 2. About Shantara

### Our story

Since 2000, Hygiene Nature Cure Hospital has welcomed people seeking a gentler, more natural way to heal. It was founded on the belief that the body has an innate ability to heal itself when given the right environment. Under Dr. P.A. Kareem's leadership, it grew into a 66-bed hospital, certified under the Kerala Clinical Establishment Act, and has treated more than 25,000 patients over the years.

Shantara Naturopathy is the evolution of that legacy, not an expansion of it but a new form of it. Spread across 4 acres of hilltop land in Kozhikode with 60,000 sq. ft. of dedicated wellness infrastructure, it blends decades of clinical expertise with mindful hospitality. The team, the philosophy, and the commitment remain the same. Only the setting has become more immersive.

### What "Shantara" means

Shantara means "A Star of Serenity."

Shantara is your guiding star, your North Star on the journey towards peace, balance, and wellbeing. It is a place to step away from the noise of everyday life, reconnect with yourself, and find your way back to a more balanced state of being.

### Our approach

Healing at Shantara begins by understanding what the body is asking for, rather than by "fixing what is broken." The approach follows naturopathy principles, in which nature leads and the body restores itself at its own pace. Each guest is treated as a whole, with physical health, mental well-being, and daily habits treated as connected. Treatment is unhurried and personalised, and it focuses on root cause rather than symptom relief.

Unlike hospitality-focused wellness resorts, Shantara operates as a clinical naturopathy retreat, where treatment efficacy depends on controlled conditions: prescribed diets, structured routines, minimal external disruption, and continuous monitoring. The house rules in [section 13](#section-13) exist to protect these clinical outcomes, not to restrict guests unnecessarily.

### Why Shantara, today

Modern illness is rarely sudden. It builds quietly through routine, stress, and neglect, and what it often needs is not intervention but interruption, meaning a pause and a space to reset. Shantara exists for that moment.

## 5. Guest Admission & Eligibility

Knowing who Shantara can and cannot treat prevents staff from promising a stay to someone the doctors will later have to turn away. When in doubt, route the enquiry to a doctor rather than confirming or declining yourself.

### Who we treat

Shantara accepts guests with chronic, lifestyle-driven conditions that are manageable through naturopathic treatment, including diabetes, hypertension, obesity, PCOD/PCOS, thyroid disorders, digestive issues, arthritis, and stress-related disorders.

This list is **clinical eligibility**, not a marketing audience model. Internal ICP definitions (need-led customer groups for SEO, campaigns, content, and CRM) live in [ICP and marketing audience](icp.html). Do not treat this admission list, or the programme names in [section 9](#section-9), as ICPs.

### Who we cannot admit

Shantara does not accept guests with critical or acute conditions requiring emergency care, intensive medical intervention, or 24/7 monitoring. This includes recent heart attacks, unstable angina, stroke, severe organ failure, active cancer under treatment, or severe psychiatric conditions requiring hospitalisation.

Our doctors reserve the right to decline admission if a guest's condition requires clinical resources beyond our naturopathic treatment capabilities.

### Age policy

Guests below 18 or above 80 years of age are not permitted to participate in programs, due to the specialised therapeutic nature of the treatments and clinical protocols involved.

Minors aged 14 to 17 may be accommodated only as non-participating companions, and only upon a doctor's recommendation.

### Pre-consultation & health assessment

Reservations are confirmed only after a mandatory preliminary consultation with our doctors. Before this consultation, the guest must submit a Preliminary Assessment or Health Assessment Form (HAF), provide all relevant medical records, and disclose all current medications. We reserve the right to decline admission if naturopathy treatment is not suitable for the guest's specific health condition.

## 7. Medical Protocols & Scope

This section defines what non-medical staff can and cannot advise on, once a guest is already at Shantara. For admission eligibility and the pre-consultation process, see [section 5](#section-5).

### Medication policy

All current medications must be declared during the preliminary consultation, and again at admission. Self-medication during the stay is strictly prohibited. Any ongoing pharmaceutical treatment continues only under the supervision of the in-house doctors, because naturopathy treatments can interact with certain medications and the doctors need complete oversight of everything entering a guest's system.

### When staff must escalate to a doctor

- Any guest-reported symptom, pain, or discomfort during a therapy session
- Any request to stop, extend, or modify a fasting or therapy protocol
- Any guest expressing distress, dizziness, or an adverse reaction to treatment or diet
- Any guest requesting to leave the property during their stay, since this requires a doctor or designated team member escort

[TO CONFIRM] Formal escalation chain and expected response time

### Scope of non-medical staff

- Non-medical staff may describe therapies, programs, and the general philosophy of naturopathy in the terms provided in this handbook.
- Non-medical staff must never diagnose, promise a health outcome, advise on medication changes, or contradict a doctor's recommendation to a guest.
- When in doubt, the correct answer is to say: "Let me check with the doctor on that for you."

## 8. Guest Privacy & Confidentiality

Guests share sensitive personal and health information in order to receive care. Protecting that information is a condition of every guest's trust in Shantara, and it is every team member's responsibility, not only clinical staff.

### What counts as confidential

- A guest's identity, presence at the property, and dates of stay
- Any health condition, diagnosis, treatment, or medication a guest discloses
- Room numbers, itineraries, and any other personal details shared with staff

### Rules for staff

- Never discuss a guest's condition, treatment, or reason for being at Shantara with anyone outside the care team. This includes other guests, the guest's own family or friends unless the guest has authorised it, media, and personal social media.
- Never confirm or deny that a specific person is a guest at Shantara to an outside caller or visitor without verified authorisation from the guest or management.
- Do not photograph or record guests without their consent, and never share guest images or details on personal social media or messaging.
- Share guest information with colleagues only when it is operationally necessary, for example a therapist passing a dietary restriction to the kitchen.

### Why this matters

Guests come to Shantara to address health conditions many would not want discussed casually. A breach of this trust affects the guest directly, and it affects Shantara's credibility as a clinical facility.

## 13. Guest Policies & House Rules

These policies protect the clinical integrity of naturopathy treatment and create an optimal healing environment. They are not arbitrary restrictions, and staff should be able to explain the reasoning behind each one if a guest asks.

### Cancellation policy

- More than 30 days before arrival: 100% refund
- 21 to 30 days before arrival: 50% refund
- 20 days or less before arrival, no-show, or early departure: no refund
- No refunds for disciplinary discharges

### Date changes

Date changes are permitted once without penalty if requested at least 20 days before the original arrival date, subject to availability. Any subsequent change is treated as a cancellation, with no refund.

### Stay duration

Most programmes are designed around a week or longer so naturopathy protocols have time to take effect. That is typical duration, not a mandatory floor for every stay: shorter stays may be offered where clinically and commercially appropriate. The guest's doctor recommends the optimal programme duration at the preliminary consultation. Early departure does not entitle a refund for unused nights of the booked stay — see the cancellation policy above. Do not quote rates here; they live on the tariff surface.

### Non-participating companions

- A companion may accompany a guest who needs a caretaker, if this is requested and approved during the preliminary consultation.
- A companion sharing the guest's room pays the nightly companion supplement shown on the current tariff. It covers meals aligned with retreat guidelines.
- A companion staying in a separate room is charged separately. The team confirms the amount.
- An extra bed or mattress is charged per bed, per night, as shown on the current tariff.
- Companions must follow all retreat policies, including diet, movement restrictions, prohibited items, attire, and conduct. Violation results in discharge without refund, and companions may not bring outside food or leave the premises.

### Helper policy

If a guest's condition requires continuous assistance, Shantara assigns a qualified nurse for 24/7 support for a daily charge that the team confirms. This must be requested during the preliminary consultation and is subject to the doctor's recommendation.

### Prohibited items

Coffee, tea, alcohol, packed food, non-vegetarian food, non-pharmaceutical drugs, cigarettes, and any other outside food are not permitted anywhere on the premises. This rule is enforced strictly, and any violation may result in discharge without refund.

### Movement during stay

Leaving the premises during the stay is not permitted. This restriction supports treatment efficacy and guest safety, since the body is in various stages of detoxification and healing that require continuous monitoring, a controlled diet, and a structured routine. In a genuine emergency requiring a guest to leave, they must be accompanied by a doctor or a designated team member.

### Digital detox

Guests are strongly advised to minimise use of mobile phones, laptops, and other communication devices, to rest the mind and support healing. If use is necessary, it should be restricted to the guest's own room so it does not disturb other guests. Devices are not permitted in treatment areas, dining areas, the library, yoga halls, meditation halls, or other shared therapeutic spaces.

### Retreat conduct

Guests are asked to follow their prescribed treatment schedule, respect quiet hours and shared spaces, converse in a low tone, and be considerate of other guests' healing journeys.

### Visitor policy

Once a guest is admitted, visitors are not allowed, to preserve an undisturbed healing environment for all guests.

### Pet policy

Pets are not permitted, to maintain a controlled therapeutic environment.

### Smoking & substance policy

Shantara is a smoke-free, alcohol-free, and drug-free facility. Possession or use of tobacco, alcohol, recreational drugs, or e-cigarettes anywhere on the premises results in immediate discharge without refund.

### Treatment protocol disclaimer

All treatments and therapies follow the doctor's prescription, and package inclusions and exclusions are specified at the time of booking. Additional therapies are available at extra cost, only after doctor's approval. Individual results vary based on health condition and adherence to the program. Naturopathy is a complementary approach and not a substitute for emergency medical care.

### Post-stay support

Guests who complete a program remain part of the Shantara family, and doctors remain available through online follow-up appointments to discuss concerns and provide continued guidance as guests update the team on their progress.

## 15. Guest Communication Guide

### How to describe Shantara in 30 seconds

> Shantara is a naturopathy wellness retreat in Kozhikode, built on more than two decades of clinical naturopathy practice. Every guest starts with a doctor consultation, and their program of treatments, therapies, and diet is personalised from there. It's a place to slow down and let the body reset, guided by medical care rather than left to chance.

### Terms staff should use

"Doctor-guided," "personalised program," "root cause," and "naturopathy protocol"

### Terms and claims staff should avoid

- Avoid promising cures or specific medical outcomes, such as saying "this will cure your diabetes." Describe support and lifestyle correction instead of guarantees.
- Avoid calling it a "hospital stay" to guests booking a wellness or retreat program. Reserve hospital framing for genuinely clinical or medical-programme contexts.
- Avoid answering specific medical questions in detail. Redirect these to the doctor instead.
- Avoid confirming a booking for a guest describing a critical or acute condition, such as a recent heart attack, stroke, or active cancer treatment. Route these to a doctor for an eligibility check first, per [section 5](#section-5).

### Common guest misconceptions

- That it is a spa or resort with optional wellness add-ons. It is the reverse: wellness is the core, and hospitality supports it.
- That they may leave the property during the stay. They may not, except for a genuine medical emergency escorted by a doctor or designated team member.
- That a site-wide 7-night minimum applies to every stay. It does not. Duration is advised following consultation; most programmes are typically designed around a week or longer.

### When to refer a question elsewhere

- Anything about diagnosis, medication, or treatment specifics should go to the doctor.
- Anything about billing or cancellation exceptions should go to front office or management.
